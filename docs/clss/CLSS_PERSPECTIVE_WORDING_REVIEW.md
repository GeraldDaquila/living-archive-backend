# CLSS Perspective-Specific Wording Review

Status: editorial proposal for review. Not deployed. Existing live nine-question strings remain unchanged until approval.

## Entry screen

**Heading:** Recognize the pattern before making the decision.

**Intro:** This guided reflection helps you examine evidence about leadership, responsibility, and the effects of authority. It does not score a person or decide whether anyone should be appointed.

**Choose the perspective for this reflection**

- **Reflect on my own leadership**
  Examine my choices, their effects, what I can learn, and what remains uncertain.
- **Reflect on another person's leadership**
  Organize evidence about another person's actions and effects without assuming their motives or reaching a verdict.

**Privacy note:** Your entries remain in this page session and are not submitted to a server.

## Self-reflection — proposed nine prompts

1. **What responsibility or decision am I facing?**  
   State the decision or role involved, why it matters now, and what is within my control.

2. **What evidence supports my current view of my own leadership?**  
   Separate what I directly did or observed, what others have told me, documented outcomes, and what I am inferring.

3. **How do I respond when resources are scarce or priorities conflict?**  
   Recall a specific trade-off, what I chose, who was affected, and what followed.

4. **What do I do when I receive difficult feedback?**  
   Consider whether I listen, seek clarification, take responsibility, make repairs, become defensive, or avoid the issue. Use an example rather than a label.

5. **What recurring effects do my choices have on other people and the wider system?**  
   Consider trust, clarity, capability, fairness, resilience, and long-term consequences—including effects I did not intend.

6. **What do my actions around authority and responsibility reveal—and what can’t I yet know?**  
   Describe behavior and consequences. Avoid treating confidence, ambition, hesitation, or reluctance as proof of motive.

7. **Have I freely accepted the responsibility, and do the people affected understand the arrangement?**  
   Consider willingness, relevant consent, expectations, and whether concerns can be raised safely.

8. **Are my authority, accountability, and review conditions clearly bounded?**  
   Identify my decision rights, limits, feedback channels, support, review date, and what happens if the arrangement is not working.

9. **What remains unverified before I make or accept this decision?**  
   Name the most important evidence gap and a fair, practical way to examine it.

## Third-party reflection — proposed nine prompts

1. **What responsibility or decision about this person's role are we facing?**  
   State the decision, the role involved, why it matters now, and who is affected.

2. **What evidence supports my current view of this person's leadership?**  
   Separate direct observation, documented outcomes, testimony or reports, reputation, and inference.

3. **How does this person respond when resources are scarce or priorities conflict?**  
   Recall a specific trade-off, what they chose, who was affected, and what followed. Note what is firsthand versus reported.

4. **What happens when this person receives difficult feedback?**  
   Look for specific examples of listening, clarification, accountability, repair, retaliation, or repeated avoidance. Do not infer a stable pattern from one incident.

5. **What recurring effects does this person's leadership have on people and the wider system?**  
   Consider trust, clarity, capability, fairness, resilience, and long-term consequences. Include system conditions that may also explain the outcome.

6. **What do this person's observable actions around authority and responsibility show—and what remains unknown?**  
   Describe behavior and consequences; do not state assumed motives as facts.

7. **Is the responsibility being freely accepted and mutually understood?**  
   Consider the person's expressed willingness, relevant consent of affected people, clarity of expectations, and whether dissent can be raised safely.

8. **Are authority, accountability, and review conditions clearly bounded?**  
   Identify scope, decision rights, feedback channels, review dates, and consequences agreed for the role.

9. **What remains unverified before a responsible decision can be made?**  
   Name the most consequential evidence gap and the fairest proportionate way to examine it.

## Shared result structure

1. **What you entered** — accurately summarize the evidence without embellishment.
2. **What appears supported** — cite the specific response(s); distinguish observation from report.
3. **What is interpretation** — label inference and provide a plausible alternative reading.
4. **What is uncertain or in tension** — preserve blanks and conflicting accounts as unresolved, not adverse findings.
5. **A bounded next step** — suggest proportionate evidence-gathering, a clarifying conversation, or a time-bound review; no verdict.
6. **Relevant doorway, if justified** — include only if a concrete need is grounded in the answers; otherwise say no doorway is warranted yet.

### Wording guardrails

- No scores, rankings, suitability verdicts, diagnosis, or moral labels.
- No unsupported claims about motives or character.
- A blank is an evidence gap, not negative evidence.
- Intention does not erase impact; impact alone does not establish intention.
- A single anecdote does not establish a recurring pattern.
- Consider system constraints and alternative explanations.
- No doorway merely to fill space.
- The self and third-party modes must remain explicitly distinct throughout the prompts and result.
