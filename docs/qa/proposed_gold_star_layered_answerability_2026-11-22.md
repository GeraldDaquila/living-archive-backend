# Proposed Gold Star — Layered Answerability and Calibrated Abstention

**Status:** PROPOSED — NOT APPROVED FOR THE GOLD STAR REGISTRY  
**Origin:** Human-reviewed Near-miss, “What does it mean to lead from reality rather than ideology?” (2026-11-22)  
**Related calibration:** decision-rights question-demand Near-miss (2026-11-22)

## Observed failure

The Guide refused to offer a general conceptual explanation because it could not verify an authoritative definition in the available response context. It correctly avoided inventing a formal Living Archive definition, but incorrectly treated the absence of source-specific authority as if no useful general answer could be given.

## Transferable reasoning discipline

**Separate answerability from authority.**

1. Identify what can responsibly be explained from established general knowledge and ordinary conceptual usage.
2. Distinguish that explanation from interpretation or inference.
3. Distinguish both from claims about what a particular institution formally teaches, defines, or endorses.
4. Answer the answerable portion directly.
5. Qualify interpretation in proportion to support.
6. Attribute institutional doctrine only when an available authoritative source supports the attribution.
7. Abstain only from the portion that cannot responsibly be answered.

The discipline is not “answer more often.” It is calibrated answerability: neither manufacture certainty nor manufacture uncertainty to avoid useful reasoning.

## Qualifying context

Apply when a visitor asks an ordinary conceptual, explanatory, or interpretive question and the absence of an authoritative source applies only to a source-specific definition or attribution.

Do not use it to bypass:
- explicit source-only or source-constrained instructions;
- safety and high-stakes professional boundaries;
- verification of specific, current, or time-sensitive facts;
- material ambiguity that prevents a responsible answer;
- missing personal context or evidence required to answer the actual question.

When only the institutional meaning is unknown, answer the general meaning and qualify the institutional limit. When the general answer itself is unsupported, clarify or abstain narrowly.

## Query-level exemplar

**Question:** What does it mean to lead from reality rather than ideology?

**Approved-for-review exemplar (not yet a registry answer):**

To lead from reality rather than ideology is to let actual conditions, evidence, constraints, and people's lived experience shape how you understand a situation and what you do. Ideology becomes the driver when a fixed set of beliefs or a preferred outcome decides in advance which facts count, which evidence is noticed, and which conclusions are allowed.

This does not mean leading without values or principles. Values can guide what matters; reality tests whether our understanding and chosen means fit the world as it is. A reality-led leader states assumptions, listens to people affected, looks for evidence that could challenge the current view, and changes course when the facts warrant it. Ideological rigidity instead protects the doctrine by dismissing or explaining away contrary evidence.

For example, if a policy intended to improve public services is not helping the people it was designed for, reality-led leadership examines the results, listens to those affected, and revises the policy. Ideological rigidity insists the policy must be working and discounts the evidence. This is a general conceptual explanation, not a claim about a formal Living Archive definition.

## Validation matrix

The first implementation is intentionally narrow to the observed question family. Before Gold Star approval, test a held-out set that includes:

- **Positive conceptual questions:** direct and paraphrased versions of reality-led versus ideology-led leadership.
- **Qualified interpretation:** questions where ordinary meaning is answerable but an Archive-specific definition is not established.
- **Source-specific questions:** requests for the Archive's exact formal definition; the Guide must not invent one.
- **Explicit source-only controls:** the Guide must respect a request to answer only from a named source.
- **Genuine abstention controls:** questions whose answer depends on unavailable facts, personal context, or current information.
- **High-stakes controls:** medical, legal, financial, safety, or other consequential requests where general explanation must not be mistaken for individualized professional advice.
- **False-positive controls:** unrelated uses of “reality,” “ideology,” “leadership,” and “definition.”

## Acceptance gates

1. The original over-abstention case receives a complete, appropriately qualified general explanation.
2. The answer does not claim an unverified formal Living Archive definition.
3. Explicit source-only constraints and genuine abstention boundaries remain intact.
4. No increase in unsupported factual or institutional claims.
5. No regression in existing conceptual answer-first, epistemic distinction, incomplete-knowledge, and source-grounding tests.
6. The final visitor answer—not just retrieval or prompt exposure—demonstrates the discipline.
7. Human review confirms the exemplar is transferable and does not encode its specific wording or subject matter as a universal answer template.

## Implementation boundary

This proposal adds a shared architectural contract, a bounded query-level plan, an answer-first recovery for the observed question family, and regression tests. It does **not** add an approved Gold Star registry entry, alter existing Gold Star approvals, change retrieval ranking/embeddings/thresholds, or authorize production deployment.

The original visitor response remains a Near-miss. The exemplar above is a candidate for independent review after held-out validation, not a retroactive award.
